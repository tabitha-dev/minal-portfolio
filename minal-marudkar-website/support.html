Areas of support

You do not need a perfect explanation.

Sometimes the first sign that support may help is simply noticing that a feeling, thought, relationship or situation keeps taking more energy than you want it to.

What we can talk about

Concerns can overlap. That is normal.

These are examples, not labels you have to fit into. A first conversation can help clarify what is most important to focus on.

•
Stress & emotional overwhelm

When responsibilities, thoughts or emotions feel difficult to carry.

•
Anxiety & excessive worrying

Persistent worry, restlessness, overthinking or difficulty feeling settled.

•
Low mood & depressive symptoms

Periods of sadness, low motivation, withdrawal or loss of interest.

•
Relationships & interpersonal difficulties

Communication, boundaries, conflict or repeated patterns with others.

•
Anger & emotional regulation

Understanding triggers and finding more workable responses to intense feelings.

•
Phobias & fears

Specific fears or avoidance that limits daily life.

•
Work stress & burnout

Exhaustion, pressure, role strain, boundaries and work-related emotional load.

•
Self-esteem & confidence

Self-doubt, negative self-talk and difficulty trusting your own judgment.

•
Life transitions & adjustment

Changes in relationships, work, education, family or life direction.

•
Personal growth & emotional well-being

Greater self-awareness, healthier patterns and a stronger sense of direction.

•
Academic challenges

Adjustment, stress, motivation and emotional concerns connected to study.

•
Career-related challenges

Career direction, uncertainty, workplace concerns and decision-making.

Support before crisis

Seeking psychological support does not need to be reserved for a crisis. Early support can create space to understand what is happening, build awareness and consider healthier ways of responding before concerns become more difficult to manage.

Something else?

If your concern is not listed here, you can still reach out and briefly describe what you are looking for. Minal can let you know whether her scope is a fit or whether a different type of professional may be more appropriate.
